# Mule floorplan

`hardware/mule/mule.kicad_pcb` is generated by `hardware/gen/gen_mule_pcb.py` from the
schematic (`hardware/gen/gen_mule.py`) and its exported netlist — see
`hardware/README.md` for the regeneration commands. This file documents the placement
decisions and the constraints a router must respect. It does not repeat the electrical
rationale already recorded in `hardware/gen/gen_mule.py` and `task-2-report.md`; it covers
layout only.

## Board

100 × 90 mm, 2 layers (F.Cu/B.Cu), everything placed on the top side.

**Height is 90 mm, not 80.** A 40-pin, 2.54 mm-pitch header (J1, J2) is inherently about
52 mm long — 19 gaps at 2.54 mm between 20 rows, a physical fact of the part, not a
footprint choice. That length has to fit in the same dimension as the isolation slot and
the isolated corner below it. At exactly 80 mm there is no arrangement of a ~52 mm header,
a ≥2.5 mm slot, and the isolated corner's own parts that leaves genuine (non-paper-thin)
DRC margin anywhere. 90 mm is the smallest round number that fits all three with real
clearance, confirmed by the DRC run below rather than assumed.

## Domain regions

The board has two regions, separated by a single straight horizontal slot:

- **Main region**, roughly Y = 0–61 mm, the full 100 mm width: everything referenced to
  DGND, +5V, or +3V3 — both IDC headers, the 17-channel inbound path (series resistors,
  clamp diodes, the three 3.3 V buffers), the 5 V outbound buffer and barcode test header,
  and the power section (barrel jack, LDO, bulk caps).
- **Isolated region**, roughly Y = 69–90 mm, X = 6–96 mm: everything referenced to ISO_5V
  or ISO_GND only — the pull-up resistors, the two opto bypass caps, the isolated screw
  terminal (J4), and the ISO test point (J7).

Three parts straddle the boundary **by design**, because the barrier they implement is
inside the package itself: the two HCPL-4661 optocouplers (U5, U6) and the isolated DC-DC
converter (U7). Their primary-side pins sit in the main region; their secondary-side pins
sit in the isolated region; the slot runs underneath the package body, between the two pin
groups.

## The isolation slot

A single closed rectangle on the Edge.Cuts layer — a real milled cutout, not a keepout
convention — spanning X = 6–94 mm, Y = 65.0–67.5 mm (2.5 mm tall, the required minimum).
It does not touch either board edge, so a bridge of solid substrate remains at both ends
and the board stays one physical piece; every component's usable area sits well inside
that span, so nothing can route around either end without also crossing the barrier's
intended separation.

**Why exactly 2.5 mm and not more.** The isolated DC-DC (TMA-0505S, SIP-7, 2.54 mm pin
pitch) is the tightest-fitting of the three straddling parts: its closest primary pin
(−Vin, pin 2) and closest secondary pin (−Vout, pin 4) are 5.08 mm apart center-to-center,
3.58 mm copper edge-to-copper edge after pad size. A 2.5 mm slot centered in that 3.58 mm
gap leaves ≈0.5 mm of clearance from each pad edge to the slot — positive, but the tightest
margin on the board, and the reason the slot is not wider: a 3 mm+ slot would not leave any
copper-to-slot clearance under U7 at all. The two HCPL-4661 packages have more room
(6.02 mm copper-to-copper across their primary/secondary columns), so the same 2.5 mm slot
gives them ≈1.8 mm of margin per side.

**Orientation.** U7's native pinout is already Y-split (pins 1/2 at local y=0/2.54, pins
4/6 at local y=7.62/12.7), so it needs no rotation to align with a horizontal slot. The
HCPL-4661s' native DIP-8 pinout is X-split (pins 1–4 in one column, 5–8 in the other), so
U5 and U6 are placed at **rot=270°** — confirmed empirically, not assumed from memory: a
standalone test board (one DIP-8, rendered, pin-1 position read back against known
reference coordinates) showed that at rot=270° the primary-side pin group (1–4) lands at
the same absolute Y as the placement origin and the secondary-side group (5–8) lands
7.62 mm further down (larger Y). Placing each package's origin so its primary row sits
~1.8 mm above the slot puts its secondary row ~1.8 mm below it, with the slot running
under the package body between them.

**What a router must respect:** no track, via, or copper pour may cross this slot, in
either direction. There is no copper path between the main and isolated regions anywhere
on the board except through the three straddling packages themselves — that is the entire
point of the barrier, and it is what the main board's own isolation regions (Task 14) reuse
at scale.

A copper/via/pour keepout rule area (`isolation_barrier`) sits over the slot too, 1 mm
taller on each side than the physical cutout (Y = 64.0–68.5 mm). The physical slot alone
already makes routing across it impossible — there's no substrate for a track to sit on —
so the keepout is belt-and-suspenders: it gives pcbnew's DRC an explicit rule to enforce on
the *approach* to the slot, not just exactly at its edge, and it renders as a clearly
visible hatched region in the pcbnew editor, a stronger visual cue than a thin cutout line
alone. Confirmed against this same DRC run: adding it introduced zero new violations.

## Headers on opposite edges

J1 (task-PC side) is on the left edge (X ≈ 8–13 mm); J2 (Pi side) is on the right edge
(X ≈ 90–95 mm). Both are bare 2×20, 2.54 mm-pitch pin headers (`Connector_PinHeader_2.54mm:
PinHeader_2x20_P2.54mm_Vertical`), not shrouded IDC box headers — deliberately: a bare
header's courtyard is 9.9 × 51.81 mm against a shrouded box header's 9.9 × 59.46 mm, and
that difference is what makes the 90 mm board height sufficient at all. It is also the
mechanically appropriate choice: J2's pins are at the real Raspberry Pi 5 GPIO physical
positions (see `hardware/gen/gen_mule.py`'s `GPIO_PHYSICAL_PIN` table), specifically so a
standard Pi ribbon cable — built to plug onto the Pi's own bare, unshrouded 40-pin header —
connects directly, without an adapter.

## Bypass-cap proximity (HCPL-4661s)

The HCPL-4661 datasheet requires a 100 nF bypass capacitor close to the package's VCC/GND
pins. C5 (serving U5) and C6 (serving U6) are placed at the midpoint between each
package's VCC (pad 8) and GND (pad 5) post-rotation positions:

| Cap | Package | Distance to VCC | Distance to GND |
|---|---|---|---|
| C5 | U5 | 4.48 mm | 4.50 mm |
| C6 | U6 | 4.48 mm | 4.50 mm |

Both comfortably inside the ~7 mm requirement. This board's purpose is measuring
optocoupler edge quality (mule bring-up checks 5–6 in the plan), so this placement was
checked by computing the actual post-rotation pin coordinates, not eyeballed — the
computation is in `hardware/gen/gen_mule_pcb.py`'s comment at the two `lay(refs["opto_bypass_c"]...)`
call sites.

Decoupling for the four 74x541 buffers (C1–C4) and the LDO (C9) is placed adjacent to its
IC but was not held to the same explicit distance target — the brief states the ~7 mm
requirement specifically for the HCPL-4661s, and only those two parts (VCC/GND on opposite
corners of the DIP-8, 11 mm apart) make single-cap placement inherently a matter of
minimizing rather than eliminating trace length to both pins.

## Back-powering through the input clamps — bring-up note

**The mule must be powered up before the task PC drives any of the 17 input lines.** Each
line's bidirectional clamp (BAT54S) ties its high side to +5V, not to a separately-fused
node — so if the task PC drives a line high while the mule's own +5V rail is unpowered (at
0 V), current flows from the task PC's output, through that line's clamp diode, into the
board's +5V net and whatever is connected to it. This is a direct consequence of the
clamp topology validated in Task 2 (see `hardware/gen/gen_mule.py`'s
`bidirectional_clamp()` docstring) and is unchanged by this layout — recorded here because
the brief specifically asks that it carry into the bring-up procedure (Task 5): power the
mule first, every time, before connecting or driving the task-PC header.

## Verification

```
kicad-cli pcb drc --exit-code-violations -o hardware/mule/drc.rpt hardware/mule/mule.kicad_pcb
kicad-cli pcb render --side top -o /tmp/mule-top.png hardware/mule/mule.kicad_pcb
```

DRC reports 210 `unconnected_items` (expected — nothing is routed yet) and 10 further
warnings, all investigated and expected, none of them a courtyard overlap:

- **8× `silk_edge_clearance`, all on U5/U6/U7's own silkscreen outline against the slot
  edge.** Inherent to straddling: a package whose body spans the barrier necessarily has
  silkscreen close to the slot boundary. Not present on any non-straddling part.
- **2× `lib_footprint_mismatch`, both on U5/U6 (the two rot=270° instances).** Investigated
  directly: a standalone board placing the identical, byte-for-byte library footprint at
  rot=0° in the same project context (same `fp-lib-table`) shows no such warning; the same
  footprint at rot=270° does. This reproduces independent of any content this generator
  adds (net assignment, added uuids) — it is specific to KiCad's own library-comparison
  check under rotation, not a defect in the embedded footprint. Zero electrical or
  mechanical consequence.

No `courtyards_overlap`, `clearance`, or `solder_mask_bridge` violations.

## What this generator does not do

It does not route any track, place any via, or add any copper fill/pour zone (beyond the
one keepout rule area over the slot, which carries no copper itself). `kicad-cli` has no
netlist-import or Specctra-DSN subcommand, so there is no headless path to routing —
placement is the deliverable; a person routes it in pcbnew and runs final DRC.
